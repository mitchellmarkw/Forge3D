Agreed 2026-11-24, in this order (from comparing Forge3D with ZBrush, Nomad, Meshmixer, HeroForge,
Blender and Lychee, and with how action figures are made):

1. **Make Part from paint** (slice 95): paint where armour, a glove or a belt goes, give a thickness, and
   get a new separate part that hugs the body. Sits on top (a fit gap) or set into the body (the body
   cut back under it, so the outline stays). Pulling an existing detail off is Split by Mask.
2. **Toy joint recipes** in Cut into Parts: butterfly shoulder, drop-down hip, ankle rocker, ab crunch,
   double elbows and knees, built from the ball, hinge and swivel pieces.
3. **Sculpt strength**: each sculpt step in the history gets a strength slider (0–150%).
4. **Lasso cut and lasso mask**: draw a shape on the screen to cut through the model there, or to mask
   everything inside it.
5. **Accessory makers**: adjustable blade, shield, staff, belt with buckle, holster, at the figure's
   scale; your own accessories can be added to the library (as with custom shapes).
6. **Proportion sliders** for a figure (head, arms, legs, shoulders, torso; heroic and chibi presets),
   and a **Reshape** box with a few handles for any shape.
7. **Print-in-place joints**: a Cut into Parts option that builds the joints captive, so the figure
   prints assembled on a filament printer.

Left out: island and suction-cup checks (the slicer does them, as it does supports).
