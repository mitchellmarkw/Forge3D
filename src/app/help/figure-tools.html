Figure, toy and kit tools

The tools for action figures, toys and scale models, roughly in the order you'd use them on a new figure. Every one can be undone, and the ones that change a shape are steps in Objects: double-click a step to change it later. Sizes are in millimetres.

Starting a figure

Figure Guide

Figure Design tab → Figure Guide

A see-through mannequin to sculpt or build to: pick a figure line's size (3.75, 5, 6, 7 or 12 inch) or type a height, and its proportions in heads (Stylised 6, Realistic 7.5, Heroic 8, Fashion 9). Pose it from a ready pose (Standing, A-pose, T-pose, Walking, Action) and then any joint. It's kept in the file but never printed.

Reference Images

View tab → Reference Images

Pictures (front, side, top...) shown behind the model, at the size you set, to trace.

Pose Figure

Figure Design tab → Pose Figure

Bends a figure sculpted in one piece (or an imported one) at its joints, the surface following. First drag the skeleton's balls onto the figure's joints (1. Fit the Skeleton), then pose it (2. Pose): a ready pose, the Figure Guide's pose, a joint's angles typed, or a ball dragged. It's a step in the history: double-click it to change the pose, and sculpt on from the posed figure. Works best on a figure made standing straight, facing the front, arms away from its sides.

Proportions

Figure Design tab → Proportions (or Pose Figure → 3. Proportions)

Make a figure sculpted in one piece heroic, chibi, or sized your way: head size, shoulder width, torso length, arm length, hand size, leg length and foot size, from 30% to 300% of how it was made. It uses Pose Figure's skeleton, placed for you: if a part comes out wrong, click 1. Fit the Skeleton and drag its balls onto the joints. It can be posed in the same step, the pose going on from the new proportions.

Scale Calculator

Tools → Scale Calculator

A real size at a scale: 6 feet at 1:12 is 152 mm.

Joints

Quick Socket

Features tab → Quick Socket

Click a part where the joint goes: a ball socket, a peg hole or a magnet pocket, sized for your printer's fit, with the matching ball, peg or stand-in magnet if you want it. Optional clicks (ridges in the cup) hold a pose.

Quick Hinge

Features tab → Quick Hinge

A single hinge, a double hinge (with a link piece) or a swivel, with a printed pin, a filament pin or a rod, and clicks if you want them.

Hand Ports

Features tab → Hand Ports

Swappable hands: a peg on the arm's end (round or shaped, chamfered, or with a ball for a ball-jointed wrist), then the socket for the peg in each hand with the same sizes. A grip hole goes through a hand for a handle: click where it goes in, and the way out to aim it. Pegs on accessories make handles or plugs. Pick the sizes from your joint standard so every hand and accessory fits, or set them and Save to Standard.

Will it stand?

Tools tab → Will It Stand? (or Pose Test → Show the balance)

The centre of weight, gold with a line down to the ground, against where the figure touches the ground: green if it stands, red if it would tip, as you pose it. It says how far it can lean before tipping. Peg Hole in Each Foot makes a hole for a stand's peg up into each sole that touches the ground. Make a Display Base... makes a round, oval, square or hexagonal base for the pose it's in, with a peg standing up under each foot (or holes for feet that have pegs), and the holes in the feet too if you like. It's made under the figure, below the plate: sit it on the plate before printing.

Linked left and right parts

Design tab → Mirror → Linked Copy on the Other Side

Build one side (the left arm), then make a linked copy mirrored across the body: whatever you do to the left arm later (resizing, sculpting, painting, a peg or a socket) shows on the right one too. Double-click the copy to move the mirror; Unlink makes it a shape of its own.

Make Hand

Figure Design tab → Make Hand

A fist, a gripping hand (wrapped round a handle of your size, with the hole through), an open, a relaxed or a pointing hand, right, left or a pair, at your figure's scale. It stands on its wrist with the socket for the wrist peg cut in, sized like Hand Ports' peg or from your joint standard. Fullness makes it chunkier. Sculpt it further if you like.

Accessories

Figure Design tab → Accessories

A blade, shield, staff or spear, belt with buckle, or holster at your figure's scale. Type sizes life size (as for a person) and pick the scale; parts are never made thinner than 0.8 mm. Grips, staffs and the shield's bar are the Handle size, the same as the grip holes of Make Hand and Hand Ports, so a gripping hand holds them. A belt goes round the waist you give (measured as printed), open at the back to clip on; a holster's loop takes that belt. The preview shows what Make adds and its printed size. Your accessories: select a shape, open Accessories and Save Selected to keep it; add it again any time at the size it was saved.

Lasso Cut

Figure Design tab → Lasso Cut

Select a shape, then drag a loop round part of it as you see it on the screen. The cut goes straight through the shape the way you're looking, so turn the view first (right-drag) to look from the best side, and drag again to draw a new loop. What to keep: cut away inside the loop (a hole or a notch right through), keep only what's inside it, or both pieces. The loop is kept as a sketch in the cut step: double-click the step to change its outline.

Cut into Parts

Figure Design tab → Cut into Parts

Cuts a figure sculpted in one piece at its joints and puts the joints in, in one go. Drag the skeleton's balls into the middle of each joint, choose where to cut (Usual Figure: ball joints at the neck, shoulders and hips, hinges at the elbows and knees) and what goes in each cut: a ball joint, a hinge, a double hinge, a swivel, one from your joint standard, or just a cut. Each cut is placed the body's way (shoulders upright, the neck and hips level, the rest square to the limb); to turn one by hand, pick its joint and set Tip forward and Tilt sideways (Straighten puts it back). The pieces are named (Torso, Left upper arm...) and the hinge pins come out beside them. Pose Test turns them at their joints.

Joint Standards

Tools → Joint Standards

A figure line's joint sizes kept by name (6 mm shoulder balls, 3 mm wrist pegs, a knee hinge...), so every figure in the line takes the same joints. In Quick Socket or Quick Hinge, set a joint up and click Save to Standard; next time pick it from the list at the top to start from its sizes. The fit stays with your printer. Export a standard to share it or back it up; Import adds one.

Design Variables

Tools tab → Variables

Named values used across many shapes (PegDia = 3, Fit = 0.1, Hole = PegDia + 2 * Fit), so changing one changes every shape that uses it. In Properties, press = in a size or position (or right-click it → Use a Variable...) and give a variable or a formula; that box turns blue. Typing a number in it, dragging it with a handle, Align, Sit on Plate, or right-click → Stop Following the Variables makes it a plain size again. Formulas may use + - * / ^, brackets, pi, min, max, abs, sqrt, round, floor and ceil. Renaming a variable carries over to everything that uses it. Deleting one leaves those sizes as they are. The sizes in Quick Socket, Quick Hinge and Hand Ports can follow them the same way: press = in a box there (both halves of a hinge follow, and Add Matching Part's ball or peg too).

Tolerance Test Print

Tools → Tolerance Test Print

Print it once: holes, cups and pin holes at stepped fits. The fit that works best becomes where Quick Socket and Quick Hinge start. With a joint standard in use, one click sizes the test to it.

Pose Test

Tools tab → Pose Test

Drag parts round their joints to see how far they move and where they bump into each other (shown red). Save poses to come back to; nothing moves for good.

Make room to turn further: choose a joint, set Turn to (a 90° knee, say) and the clearance, and Make Room trims away just what's in the way, from the part it hits or from the part that turns. Undo puts it back.

Detail

Texture stamps

Sculpt tab → Sculpt → Pattern brush

Press a texture into the surface where you drag: scales, hexagons, woven cloth, twill, knitting, chainmail, leather, skin, skin pores, bricks, cracked stone or wood grain, or a picture of your own (light parts stand highest). Choose Once, as a stamp to press a picture once where you click (an emblem, a scar, a patch); Turned turns it round on the surface.

Stamp

Design tab → Stamp

Rivets, bolts, screws, studs, vents, scales, stitches or your own custom shapes, raised or cut in. Click for one, drag for a row.

Panel Lines

Design tab → Panel Lines

Click points along the surface to cut a thin V, square or round groove that follows it. End Line starts another; Close Loop goes round a panel.

Straight lines: in both, hold Shift to keep the line level (the same height all the way) or Ctrl to keep it upright as you see it; it still follows the surface.

Getting it ready to print

Print Check

Tools tab → Print Check

Colors places too thin to print well red, and faces that will need supports blue, the way the parts sit now.

Hollow

Features tab → Hollow

For resin: takes the inside out, leaving walls of a set thickness, with drain holes (clicked, or Place Them for Me) and plugs to close them. Works on sculpts too.

Make Mold

Print/Save tab → Make Mold

A mold box round a part, for casting resin copies of a printed master. Choose the Split direction (horizontal: top and bottom; vertical: left and right, or front and back) and where the cut goes (Where It's Widest to start). Undercuts, where a piece would catch on the part, show red with how much there is; Find Best Split picks the direction and place with the least. For a part that still catches, make 3 or 4 pieces: a second cut another way through one half or both. Keys line the pieces up; the spout (red, with a funnel) runs from the part's top up out of the mold, and air vents from its other high spots (Place Vents, or click the part to add one). Print each piece with its cut face down. Double-click any piece to change them all.

Sculpt Strength

Click a sculpt step → Properties → Sculpt strength

Tones a whole sculpt step down or pushes it further, every stroke together: 100% is as you sculpted it, 50% is halfway, 0% is as before the step, up to 150% for more. The strokes are kept, so 100% always gives back exactly what you sculpted. Good for a face that came out too strong, or muscles you'd like a little bolder.

Reshape

Sculpt tab → Reshape

A box of gold handles round the selected shape: drag a handle and the shape bends smoothly with it, like pulling on a cage round it. Corners only stretch and lean it; 3 × 3 × 3 bulges, pinches and bends; 4 × 4 × 4 shapes smaller areas. With Move left and right together each handle's partner moves the mirror way. Handles move across the screen, so turn the view to move one the other way. It's a step: double-click it to move the handles again.

Simplify

Sculpt tab → Simplify

Fewer triangles for a heavy sculpt: by a percentage or a number of triangles, with the STL file size it makes.

Paint

Design tab → Paint

Brush colors onto one shape (skin, clothes, eyes) for a color printer: ready colors or any other, Fill All for the base color, Mirror left-right for both sides at once, Ctrl to rub out. The colors show in the view, stay with the shape when it moves or is rescaled, and go into 3MF exports (read by Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer and PrusaSlicer as painted filaments). Paint after posing: a pose added afterwards bends the shape but leaves the paint where it was.

Split by Mask (split for painting)

Design tab → Split by Mask

Paint the part to cut off (a hand, a belt, a head) and cut along the paint's edge. Alignment keys on the cut let the painted pieces glue back only one way. Cut surface chooses the cut: Curved follows the paint's edge; Flat cuts flat through the middle of it; Ball and socket cuts flat with a dome on the painted piece and a socket (Socket fit bigger) in the rest, so they sit together one way and turn on it. Flat and ball and socket need the paint's edge to go round the shape once (a hand, not a band round the middle). Joint at the cut puts a joint in where the pieces meet (see below). On a shape painted with colors, "Mask from a painted color" cuts one color off as its own piece.

Toy joint recipes

Figure Design tab → Cut into Parts → Joint type

The joints toy figures use, each with a small block between two joints: Butterfly shoulder (an upright hinge in the chest, then the ball, so the arms swing forward), Drop-down hip (a hinge so the hip's ball drops for high kicks), Ankle rocker (the foot up and down, and side to side), and Ab crunch (a swivel, then a hinge so the chest bends forward). Super Poseable chooses them all, with double elbows and knees.

Lasso Masks

Split by Mask and Make Part: Lasso: draw a loop instead. Sculpt: Lasso mask

Instead of brushing, drag a loop round the part: everything inside it is painted (or masked, when sculpting), right through the shape, front and back. A loop round a hand paints all of the hand. Hold Ctrl to rub out inside the loop. Loops and brush strokes can be mixed; Undo Stroke takes back the last loop.

Make Part

Figure Design tab → Make Part

Paint where armor, a glove, a boot or a belt goes, and a new part is made there that follows the body. It sits on top with a small Fit gap, or is set into the body: the body is cut back under it so the outline stays the same and the part sits flush. The part is a shape of its own, to sculpt, paint, or hold on with magnets or a peg. (To pull an existing detail off as its own piece, use Split by Mask.)

A joint at a split

Design tab → Split, or Split by Mask → Joint at the cut

Instead of alignment pegs, put a joint where the two pieces meet: a Ball joint (a snap-in socket in one piece, the ball on a neck on the other, the other piece cut back a little so the ball can tilt), a Hinge, a Double hinge (with a link), a Swivel (a peg turning in a bore) or Magnets (a pocket in each face). It goes at the middle of the cut, the spot farthest from its edges. Size "Fit the cut" sizes it to how thick the cut is; Joint fit is the room round the moving parts (0.1 mm for resin, 0.2 for filament). With a plane split the socket (or fork, bore) goes in the side the arrow points to; with a mask, in the painted piece. The joint follows its cut: Move the cut... (also from inside the shape's history) or change the mask, and the joint is put in again in the middle of the new cut, sized to fit it.

Rotate for Best Print

Print/Save tab → Rotate for Best Print (or Tools tab → Place)

Turns the selected shapes the best way up to print and rests them on the plate. Fastest Print keeps it low with little support; Best Quality keeps supports and layer lines off as much of the surface as it can; Strongest Print lays it along its length, so the layers run along a limb or blade rather than across it (parts break between layers). Choose your Printer: on Resin every layer takes the same time (so Fastest is just the lowest), and a big layer pulls hard on the film, so Best Quality usually tips the part rather than laying it flat. Turn it to take the least room then turns it on the plate so it takes as little room as it can, its long side left to right. The window shows how it compares with how it sits now before you click Rotate.

Rescale Design

Tools → Rescale Design

Move a design to another scale (1:12 to 1:18), a height or a percentage, keeping joints, pegs and magnet pockets at their real size, and hollow walls as thick, if you want.

Kits and planning

Instruction Sheet

Print/Save tab → Instruction Sheet (or Tools → Instruction Sheet)

A sheet to go with a kit: the parts pulled apart (an exploded view), drawn like a kit's instructions with a numbered balloon on each part, the parts list under it, and your notes for putting it together. The numbers are the Parts List's (it now has a # column). Choose the view (front, three-quarter, a side, the back or the top), how far the parts are pulled apart, whether the parts inside a kit or a joined shape are shown, and Letter or A4; then Save as PDF. Select shapes first to show only those.

Sewing Pattern

Print/Save tab → Sewing Pattern (or Tools → Sewing Pattern)

Cloth clothes for a figure. Paint where the garment goes with Paint (a jacket, a cape, trousers), one color for it, then select the figure and click Sewing Pattern. It's cut into pieces at the seams you choose (front and back with side seams, left and right with a seam down the middle, or one piece for something that already lies open) and each piece is laid flat at full size, the way up the figure up the page (the grain arrow). The solid line is where to cut (with the seam allowance you set); the dashed line is where to sew. It says how close lengths stay to the figure's: a place that can't lie flat (a tight armpit) is better with a seam there: paint a thin line across it in another color and tick Also cut along lines painted (with One piece, it's cut only along your lines). Little gaps in the paint count as painted. Save as PDF to print at 100% (bigger patterns go over several pages; check the 50 mm square), or as SVG for a cutting machine.

Picture

Print/Save tab → Picture (or Tools → Picture)

A clean picture of the model for posts or a shop listing: smoothly lit in a studio light, in its colors and paint, with a soft shadow on the ground, on white, light grey, dark, or no background at all. Choose how far round it's seen from and how far you're looking down, and how much of the picture it fills. Save a PNG (1080 square for posts, 2000 square for a shop, tall, wide or small), or choose A turntable to save a GIF of it going once round (choose how many pictures in the turn and how long it takes; tick "Turn the preview" to see it first). The selected shapes, or everything. For a posed figure, use Picture of This Pose... in Pose Test.

Show and Hide

Objects list → the eye by a shape; View → Hide Selected (Ctrl+H), Show All (Ctrl+Shift+H)

Click a shape's eye to hide it from the view, and again to show it: handy to see a part under another, or the cut face of one piece of a split. Right-click a shape for Show Only This and Show All. Hiding only changes what you see: a hidden shape is still saved, exported and printed, and everything shows when a file is opened.

Kit Layout

Tools → Kit Layout

Small parts in rows on a frame like a model kit's sprue: each on a thin gate with a numbered tag, and the tree's name on a tag under the frame. Joined into one shape to print, or not.

Parts List

Tools → Parts List

Every part's size, volume, print weight and cost for your material, copied into a spreadsheet or saved as CSV or a PDF.
